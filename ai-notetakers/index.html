Your client calls deserve notes you trust. Pick the AI notetaker that fits them.

Consultants, coaches, recruiters and solo sales people record client calls on Zoom, Teams and Meet to get summaries and action items afterwards. Otter dominates the category — and the complaints about it are specific: multi-speaker accuracy when people talk over each other, language coverage, a visible bot joining client calls, and minute limits on the free tier. This page is the boring version of that debate: what people actually complain about, what to check before you switch, the tools whose prices we read on their own pages today, and who pays you to switch.

In brief

  • Otter: free tier capped at 300 min/month; Pro 8.33 $/user/month billed annually (16.99 monthly).
  • Fathom: individual plan free with unlimited recordings; Team 19 $/user/month, 2-user minimum.
  • Fireflies: named first for CRM integrations (HubSpot, Salesforce); its affiliate pays 30% recurring for 12 months.
  • The bot question: default capture joins as a visible guest — bot-free options exist (Tactiq extension; Fathom beta on Mac), pricing on those two not verified.

What people actually complain about

From the r/NoteTaking discussion “Anyone switched from Otter AI? Looking for better alternatives” (linked in Sources) — the repeat themes, in the words of the people switching:

“The biggest issues were language limitations and accuracy especially when conversations got messy with people talking over each other. It just needed too much cleanup afterward to be useful.”— r/NoteTaking, “Anyone switched from Otter AI?” · checked 1 Oct 2026
  • Multi-speaker accuracy. The complaint that starts the switch — quoted above in the switcher’s own words — and, shorter: “The multi-speaker accuracy was the biggest pain point for me.”
  • Language coverage. Mixed-language client conversations came up repeatedly; people working across languages said English-first tools degraded quickly the moment a call switched languages.
  • The bot in the room. “A bot join every call got old fast.” On client and sales calls a visible AI-guest is a social and trust issue, not just a technical one — several users said they switched for exactly that reason.
  • Free-tier limits. Otter’s own pricing page (checked 1 Oct 2026): Basic is free with 300 monthly transcription minutes. For anyone with several client calls a week, that is the wall people hit.

Those are reasons to evaluate, not automatic reasons to switch: the line-up below includes genuinely free tiers and no-bot options, and the checklist tells you what to check first.

What to check before switching — 5 steps

1 · Countyour real minutes per month against free caps — Otter Basic: 300 min.
2 · The botwill a visible AI guest bother your clients? One answer filters the field.
3 · Test messyscore accuracy on a real call — over-talking, accents, mixed languages — not a demo.
  1. Count your real meeting volume. Minutes per month across Zoom/Teams/Meet, compared against free-tier caps (Otter Basic: 300 transcription minutes/month, verified 1 Oct 2026). If you are under the cap, the “limit” complaint may not bite you at all.
  2. Decide the bot question up front. Will a visible AI bot joining the call bother your client? On sales and discovery calls, many users say yes — and that single answer filters the field (bot-based capture vs browser-extension or local tools, and Fathom’s bot-free capture, in beta on Mac).
  3. Score the two features that drive the switch. Multi-speaker accuracy in messy conversations and language coverage are the two complaints that actually appear in the thread. Test each candidate on a real call, not a demo call.
  4. Check the integrations you already pay for. CRM (HubSpot, Salesforce), Slack, Notion: which tool writes its summaries into the places you already look? Fireflies is the one thread users most often name for CRM integrations.
  5. Price at your own seat count on the official page. Individual and team plans differ sharply (Fathom: free for individuals, Team at $19/user/month with a 2-user minimum; Otter Business at $19.99/user/month billed annually — both verified 1 Oct 2026). Questions

Why are people switching away from Otter?

The repeat complaints in the source thread: multi-speaker accuracy when people talk over each other, language coverage, a visible bot joining client calls, and free-tier minute limits. Otter’s own pricing page (checked 1 Oct 2026) caps Basic at free with 300 monthly transcription minutes.

Is there a free AI meeting notetaker without a minute cap?

Yes — Fathom’s individual plan is free with unlimited recordings and transcriptions (fathom.ai/pricing, checked 1 Oct 2026). Thread users describe it as essentially solving the limit issue versus Otter. Its default capture still joins the call as a guest bot; bot-free capture is in beta on Mac.

Which AI notetaker does not join the call as a bot?

Browser-extension tools capture the meeting audio directly from the tab instead of joining as a guest — Tactiq is the one most often named in the thread for Zoom/Meet. Fathom also offers bot-free capture, currently in beta on Mac only. We did not verify Tactiq’s current pricing at publish time.

What if I mostly take notes from in-person meetings?

People in the thread with that mix switched to hardware recorders (e.g. Plaud, HiDock) for conversations in the same room, sometimes combined with an app for video calls. That is a different buying decision — verify device costs and privacy claims on the vendor pages before comparing.
