Kit raised prices for the first time in 12 years. Breathe — then check your actual costs.

On 8 September 2025, Kit (formerly ConvertKit) announced its first price increase since 2013. If you run a newsletter, coach, or a small service or e-commerce business with anywhere from 500 to 50,000 contacts, you may be weighing a switch. This page is the boring version of that debate: what actually changed, what to check before you move, the platforms whose prices we read on their own pages today, and who pays you to switch.

What actually changed

From Kit’s own letter to its creator community (linked in Sources), the facts — not the panic:

  • First increase in 12 years. Founder Nathan Barry: “Let me get straight to it: we’re raising prices for the first time in 12 years.” Prices had stood since the product was priced in 2013.
  • New customers: the new prices took effect on the announcement day, 8 September 2025.
  • Existing customers: nothing changes until the first billing date after 15 October 2025, with at least 30 days’ notice before anything shifts on the account.
  • What this is not: a surprise charge landing tomorrow. Current customers were grandfathered for more than a month, and the company kept a support route for anyone materially affected.

That last point matters when you read the angry threads: the price change is real, and the timing is known in advance. The decision you actually face is whether your list size and feature use justify the new number — which is what the checklist below is for.

What to check before switching — 5 steps

  1. Read your renewal date first. Price increases for existing customers usually bite at the next billing cycle (Kit’s: after 15 Oct 2025). You almost always have weeks, so a switch should never be a same-day panic move.
  2. Size your list against the free tiers. Kit’s Free plan covers up to 10,000 subscribers at $0/month (verified 1 Oct 2026). At under 10k contacts, the “Kit hike” may cost you exactly nothing.
  3. List the features you actually use — forms, automations and welcome sequences, paid subscriptions, landing pages, the integrations that touch your other tools. Compare those, not the brochure headline features.
  4. Test the import before committing. Providers import subscriber CSV exports, but double opt-in resets are normal, and design/automation migration is manual work. Run a real test with a sample list.
  5. Price at your own size on the official page. Email pricing is band-based (per-subscriber or per-contact). A blog screenshot of one band is not your price — the vendor’s page is.

Who pays you to switch

This need exists because price hikes push people to look, and the platforms know it: several run referral programs built on exactly this moment. Verified on official pages:

  • Kit: “50% commission for 12 months, plus 10-20% recurring revenue beyond 12 months” (Bronze/Silver/Gold tiers by referred paying customers; kit.com/affiliates, checked 1 Oct 2026).
  • GetResponse: “up to 60% recurring commission” per paying referral, tiered (BRONZE starts at 40% for 12 months; getresponse.com/affiliate-programs, checked 1 Oct 2026).
  • beehiiv: up to 60% commission every month, per its program page (checked 30 Sep 2026).

Questions

Did Kit really raise its prices?

Yes — the first increase in 12 years, announced by founder Nathan Barry on 8 September 2025. New customers saw new prices that day; existing customers keep their price until their first billing date after 15 October 2025.

What does Kit cost now?

Checked 1 Oct 2026 on kit.com/pricing: Free $0/mo up to 10,000 subscribers; Creator $33/mo ($390/yr); Pro $66/mo ($790/yr) — Creator/Pro shown for the example of 1,000 subscribers. Confirm at your own size on the page.

Should I switch because of the price increase?

Not automatically. Check your list size against free tiers first (Kit’s free tier: up to 10,000 subscribers), write down the features you actually use, and read your renewal date — increases usually bite at renewal, so you have time to decide properly.

What is the honest bottom line?

At small list sizes the Kit change may cost you nothing (free tier up to 10k). At larger sizes, the decision comes down to feature parity and your own price band — computed on the official page, not on a screenshot.
