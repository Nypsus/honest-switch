Stop retyping invoices from PDFs by hand.

If your team still retypes invoice data from PDFs and email attachments into accounting or ERP systems, you are paying twice: once for software, once in hours. Two technologies get mixed up here. OCR turns a page — scan or photo — into raw characters; it reads text, not meaning. AI extraction adds understanding: models locate the fields you actually need and return them as structured data you can push into a spreadsheet or ERP — how well depends on your documents, so test on your own files. Below: the tools, what they charge, and the free route we could stand behind.

In brief

  • Docparser: from $32.50/mo billed annually (100 credits; 1 credit = 1 document up to 5 pages).
  • Parseur: free up to 20 pages/mo; $49/mo for 100 pages — email-first, no code.
  • Nanonets / Airparser: $100/mo (100 credits) and $33/mo (100 credits, annual).
  • Open source: $0 — invoice2data + Camelot (MIT), text PDFs only.
  • Rossum: from $18,000/year — enterprise contract, quote only.

The free route we checked: open source

At low volume, with text-based PDFs, the honest $0 option is a Python stack: invoice2data (MIT licence) for invoice fields via templates, and Camelot (MIT licence) for tables. Limits, stated plainly: Camelot and Tabula “only work with text-based PDFs” (add your own OCR for scans); invoice2data needs a template per supplier layout; and you get no hosted UI, support contract, mailbox ingestion or security paperwork. Free like self-hosting is free.

Before you buy: the 3 questions

1 · Volumecount the credits you really consume — a 3-page PDF = 3 credits, an email = 1.
2 · Formatstext PDFs, scans, photos? Test your worst scan — support varies widely.
3 · Exitcheck exports, retention, and what happens when credits run out before committing.
  1. Volume — and how is it counted? Most vendors count credits per page or per document (Parseur: an email = 1 credit, a 3-page PDF = 3 credits); Nanonets counts per block-run in a workflow.
  2. Formats. Text PDFs, scans, photos, emails? Check the vendor’s supported list and test your worst scan — support varies widely.
  3. Where does the data land? Which integrations/API, how long documents are kept, and where they are processed (data residency) — relevant with client data.

5-step rollout

  1. Pick one or two document types with clear pain.
  2. Collect 20–50 real samples, including your worst scans.
  3. Run the shortlisted tools’ free trials on the same samples; score field-level accuracy yourself.
  4. Check the exit: exports/API, retention limits, and what happens mid-month when credits run out.
  5. Put one workflow into production for 30 days; compare time saved against plan cost before scaling.

Questions

OCR or AI extraction — which do I need?

OCR only reads characters; to get named fields in a spreadsheet you need extraction on top. Most modern tools bundle both.

Do I need a developer?

Not necessarily — Parseur, Airparser and Docparser run on templates and clicks. Mindee is API-first; Rossum and Docsumo are sales-led.

What happens when I run out of credits?

It varies: Parseur and Airparser stop processing until renewal (Parseur also sells top-up credits); Nanonets is pay-as-you-go. Know this before month-end.

Can they read scanned PDFs and photos?

Support differs by vendor. Airparser states OCR support for images, scanned documents and handwritten text; our open-source pick cannot handle scans at all. Verify on your worst documents.
