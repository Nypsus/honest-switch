Xero raised US prices on 1 October 2026. Check your actual bill before switching.

On 1 October 2026, Xero’s US plans moved from $25/$55/$90 to $27/$59/$97 a month. For some businesses that’s a shrug; for others on Established it’s the third annual increase in a row — a legitimate reason to re-shop your books. This page is the boring version of that debate: what changed, what to check before you move, alternatives with prices we verified ourselves, and the catches nobody mentions.

In brief

  • Xero US: Early $27/mo, Growing $59/mo, Established $97/mo — up $2/$4/$7 on 1 Oct 2026.
  • Zoho Books: free tier (revenue under $50K/yr, up to 1,000 invoices); paid Standard at $15/mo annual.
  • Wave: Starter free; Pro $19/mo — card payments extra (2.9% + $0.60).
  • FreshBooks / QuickBooks: Lite $23/mo; Simple Start $38/mo — QuickBooks raised its own prices in Aug 2026 too.

  • Small numbers, real direction. The US accounting firm Numerawise, which published a before/after table verified on 27 August 2026, puts it plainly: for some businesses this is a shrug, for others it’s the third increase in as many years.
  • New customers: the official page currently offers “80% off your plan for your first 3 months” ($5.40/$11.80/$19.40), then Xero’s regular price applies and the subscription auto-renews monthly.
  • Payroll is separate. Xero’s payroll (powered by Gusto) is an optional add-on billed on top of the plan, with a monthly price plus a per-employee fee — most rivals’ payroll is an add-on too. Price the whole stack, not just the plan.
  • What this is not: anyone’s exact bill. Your own next charge date and amount live on your Billing page in Xero — read that, not a blog chart.

That last point matters when you read the angry threads: the increase is real and dated, but the only number that decides anything is the one on your next invoice. If it hasn’t changed yet, you have time to decide properly.

What to check before switching — 5 steps

1 · Your billread your Xero Billing page — plan + next charge date, not a blog chart.
2 · Size itthe delta is $2–$7 a month — it alone rarely pays for a migration.
3 · Whole stackcompare including payroll and add-ons, at your own size, on official pages.
  1. Read your Xero Billing page first. It shows your plan and next charge date. Increases land on billing dates, so if your next invoice still shows the old price, you have weeks — a switch should never be a same-day panic move.
  2. Size the actual raise. We’re talking $2, $4 or $7 a month. Ask whether the increase alone breaks anything, or whether the real problem is being on Established while using it like Early — that overpay existed before this change.
  3. List the features you actually use: bank feeds, invoicing, inventory, multicurrency, the payroll add-on, integrations that touch your other tools. Compare those, not the brochure headline features.
  4. Export before you cancel anything. Xero exports to CSV/Excel and most tools import from those. Run the new tool in parallel for at least one clean invoicing and reconciliation cycle, then cancel.
  5. Price at your own size on official pages — including add-ons. A screenshot of one band is not your price; the vendor’s pricing page is, and payroll on top counts.

Questions

Did Xero really raise its US prices?

Yes. On Xero’s official US pricing page, checked 1 October 2026: Early $27/month, Growing $59/month, Established $97/month — up from $25, $55 and $90. The change took effect 1 October 2026 and is documented in a before/after table by the US accounting firm Numerawise (verified 27 August 2026).

Do I pay the new price right away?

Check your own Billing page: it shows the current plan and next charge date. Xero subscriptions auto-renew monthly until cancelled, per the official page. If your next invoice still shows the old amount, you have time to decide.

Is the increase big enough to justify switching?

The monthly delta is $2 to $7. That alone rarely justifies a migration — but being on a tier you don’t use, or facing a third annual increase, might. Do the checklist, then decide; a price rise alone is a nudge, not a verdict.

What is the cheapest credible alternative?

Verified 30 Sep 2026 on official pages: Zoho Books free tier (revenue under $50K/yr, up to 1,000 invoices) and Wave’s free Starter. Payroll and card payments cost extra on both — price the whole stack.

What is the honest bottom line?

For most businesses the Xero increase is small. It becomes a real reason to switch only when you weren’t using your tier anyway, or when an alternative covers what you actually use for less — computed on official pages, at your own size.
